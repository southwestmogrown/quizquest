# Meet the AI Coach

Every lesson on QuizQuest has an AI coach built in. On code challenges and quizzes, look for the **I'm stuck** button. On reading lessons, look for **Ask the Coach**. Either way, you get a conversation with an AI that knows the lesson content.

But this coach works differently from most AI assistants you've used.

## Socratic Mode, Not Answer Mode

Most people's instinct when stuck is to ask the AI for the answer. QuizQuest's coach is explicitly designed to refuse this — not because it can't answer, but because giving you the answer doesn't help you learn.

Instead, the coach asks you questions. It tries to figure out where your understanding breaks down and guide you to the answer yourself. This is the Socratic method: learning through dialogue rather than instruction.

It might feel frustrating at first. That friction is the learning.

## What the Coach Actually Does

When you open the coach on a code challenge, it sees your current code. It can tell what you've tried. A typical exchange:

> **You:** I don't know where to start.
>
> **Coach:** What does the expected output look like? Walk me through what each line is saying.

> **You:** It says "Learner: Alex" on the first line.
>
> **Coach:** What Go function do you know that prints text to the screen?

It nudges, it doesn't carry. By the time you hit Submit, you understand why the code works — not just that it works.

## Why This Matters

The difference between a learner who can recall a concept and one who can apply it under pressure comes down to whether they ever had to struggle through it themselves. Being handed the answer bypasses that struggle entirely.

The coach is there to make the struggle productive, not to end it prematurely.

## What the Coach Won't Do

- Write your code for you
- Paste working solutions
- Explain concepts by showing you the answer in code form

It will guide, question, hint, and clarify. That's it.

## On Reading Lessons

On reading lessons like this one, the coach switches to Q&A mode. Ask it anything about the lesson content — it will answer directly, without the Socratic scaffolding. The goal on reading lessons is comprehension, not problem-solving.
